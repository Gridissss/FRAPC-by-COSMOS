01 — How it works

From satellite to sidewalk, in four steps.

Keep scrolling: the kiosk on the side follows every step.

01

A radar that sees through the rain

Under Amazon clouds, ordinary imaging satellites go blind. Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) doesn't: it reads soil moisture and ground movement even in the middle of a storm. Today we use Sentinel-1; the project was designed for NASA's L-band NISAR, which picks up millimetre-scale shifts on slopes.

  • Terrain and slope
  • Lowland elevation
  • 500 × 500 m grid
02

The rain right now — and over the next hours

Every minute the system fetches measured and forecast rainfall for the kiosk's exact spot (Meteomatics, with Open-Meteo as backup) and adds up what fell over 1, 6, 24 and 72 hours. Ground that has been soaked for three days slides with far less rain than dry ground.

  • 1 h → 72 h totals
  • 5-day forecast
  • Cached, so it never goes dark
03

A model trained on what already happened

A LightGBM model learned from Civil Defense incident history which mixes of rain, terrain and radar ended in floods or landslides. Each cell's probability becomes one of four levels — and a neighbourhood's level only goes up when the risk covers a real part of it.

  • Calm · Watch · Danger · Extreme
  • Threshold tuned on F1
04

An answer anyone can understand

The kiosk shows your neighbourhood and the ones around it on a satellite map and, when it's time to leave, draws the walking route to the nearest hospital, fire station or safe area — always in a neighbourhood that is calm. If yours is flooding, it won't send you to the hospital down the same street.

  • No touch: automatic zoom
  • Voice only during alerts

02 — Why Manaus

From January to May, the Amazon rainy season doesn't ask permission.

0k

people live on slopes classified as high risk in the city, according to the Census. The Mindu, Educandos and São Raimundo streams fill up in minutes; soaked through, the soil of the Alter do Chão Formation gives way without a sound.

The warning has to be where people are — at the bus stop, in the square, at the school gate. Not in an app nobody opened.

04 — Four levels, four actions

Nobody needs to understand rain in millimetres. Just colours.

  1. Calm

    You can stay home.

    The kiosk stays quiet and shows your neighbourhood and the ones next to it. It doesn't send anyone away.

  2. Watch

    It may happen. Get ready.

    Keep your bag packed. The kiosk already shows where to go, in case you need to leave.

  3. Danger

    It will happen. Leave home.

    A walking route to a hospital, fire station or safe area in a calm neighbourhood. Voice and alarm tone.

  4. Extreme

    It's happening. Leave now.

    A flashing banner, Civil Defense numbers up front and the shortest way out of harm's reach.

05 — Built for the street, not the showroom

Small decisions that make a difference on the day.

Every kiosk knows where it stands

The kiosk finds its neighbourhood from the official OpenStreetMap boundary and also looks at the ones that share a border. The same screen in Educandos and in Cidade Nova shows different realities.

Nobody has to touch it

Automatic zoom: the map opens on the whole neighbourhood, then moves in on the route. No smudged or frozen screens.

Speaks only when it matters

On a calm day, silence. Voice and alarm tone come in at watch, danger and extreme.

Won't send you into another flood

A hospital only counts if its own neighbourhood is calm. Too far? The kiosk points to the nearest safe area.

Copes with bad internet

Neighbourhoods and support points are stored on the device. If the connection drops, the kiosk says so and recovers on its own.

Manifesto · FRAPC Cosmos

While satellites orbit the silence of space, FRAPC Cosmos listens to the heart of Manaus. We bring NASA technology down from the stars, straight to the city's most vulnerable areas. More than decoding radar data, we deliver relief and hope. Because every alert sent in time is a whole story that wasn't erased.

06 — Under the hood

Open data, an explainable model, open source.

  1. ASatelliteSAR Sentinel-1 · NISAR L-band · terrain
  2. BRainMeteomatics · Open-Meteo · 1 h to 72 h
  3. CModelLightGBM · Civil Defense records
  4. DKioskFastAPI · Leaflet map · Esri imagery

What goes in

  • SAR radar · VV / VH backscatter
  • Digital elevation model · height and slope
  • Hourly rainfall, measured and forecast
  • Historical Civil Defense records
  • Neighbourhoods, hospitals and fire stations · OpenStreetMap

What comes out

  • Flood and landslide level for each neighbourhood
  • Next 5 hours, tomorrow and the next 4 days
  • Where to go and how long it takes on foot
  • One screen per kiosk, refreshed every 15 s
  • Open API: GET /totem?lat=&lng=
